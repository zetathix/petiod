# PetOID Theoretical Framework & Notation (v2)

The PetOID framework defines synthetic life through input/output constraints, honesty indices, and physical/operational friction vectors. It serves to classify, evaluate, and trace the evolutionary lifespan of artificial creatures, software pets, and robotic companions.

---

## 1. Core Notation: `PetI:O!D`

Synthetic entities are classified using the structural input-to-output expression **`PetI:O!D`**:

* **`I` (Input Scale):** The complexity and frequency of sensory or physical inputs the entity accepts from the environment/user.
* **`O` (Output Scale):** The expressiveness, physical movement, or feedback loops generated by the entity.
* **`!D` (Decay & Noise Factor):** The internal accumulator of unprompted decay, random drift, or noise. An entity with `!D` has an autonomous internal state that shifts independently of direct user input.

---

## 2. Deprivation Weight & Taxon Lifespan Decay ($L_t$)

The operational lifespan and historical survival of a synthetic species (*Taxon Age*, denoted as $L_t$) is inversely proportional to its **Deprivation Weight ($W_d$)**. High maintenance burdens and external dependencies generate **friction decay**, causing users to abandon, power off, or store the entity, leading to functional extinction.

### The 6-Vector Deprivation Weight Formula

$$W_d = \underbrace{(w_{\text{phys}} + w_{\text{pwr}} + w_{\text{maint}} + w_{\text{rout}})}_{\text{Internal / Human Friction}} + \underbrace{(w_{\text{tech}} + w_{\text{resp}})}_{\text{External / Infrastructure Drag}}$$

#### Internal / Human Friction Vectors
1. **Physical Dimension Drag ($w_{\text{phys}}$):** Mass, spatial footprint, and portability friction.
2. **Power & Energy Dependency ($w_{\text{pwr}}$):** Battery degradation, charging frequency, or dependence on proprietary power docks.
3. **Hardware & Material Maintenance ($w_{\text{maint}}$):** Fragility of mechanical joints, gear stripping, skin wear, and material degradation.
4. **Routine & Tamagotchi-Tethering ($w_{\text{rout}}$):** The psychological burden of mandatory user intervention (e.g., strict feeding/cleaning timers that punish the user).

#### External / Infrastructure Drag Vectors
5. **Technological Pace Drag ($w_{\text{tech}}$):** Host architecture obsolescence, deprecated OS runtimes, or discontinued microcontrollers rendering the entity unrunnable without artificial emulation.
6. **Responsibility Contract & Cloud-Supply Drag ($w_{\text{resp}}$):** Reliance on active cloud servers, subscription APIs, proprietary authentication pipelines, or centralized spare-part supply chains.

---

## 3. Taxon Lifespan Decay ($L_t$) Equation

The expected historical persistence (Taxon Age $L_t$) of an entity family in the wild is governed by its **Honesty Index ($H$)** relative to its **Deprivation Weight ($W_d$)**:

$$L_t \propto \frac{H}{W_d}$$

* **High $H$, Low $W_d$ (Maximum Survival):** Self-contained, low-maintenance entities (e.g., `NEKO.COM`, Qoobo) require no external cloud servers ($w_{\text{resp}} = 0$) and zero mandatory routine upkeep, achieving near-infinite species persistence ($L_t \rightarrow \infty$).
* **Low $H$, High $W_d$ (Rapid Extinction):** Dishonest or over-engineered entities (e.g., cloud-dependent social robots) require constant server pinging, proprietary docks, and high maintenance. When servers sunset or hardware breaks, species death occurs rapidly ($L_t \rightarrow 0$).

## 5. Binomial Nomenclature Rules

Species naming within the framework follows a modified Linnaean structure:

* **Genus Name:** Refers to the recognized archetype, brand line, or project lineage (e.g., *Tamagotchi*, *Neko*, *Anas*).
* **Specific Epithet (Tail Name):** Latin descriptive adjectives indicating structural traits or chronological placement:
  * `initialis` / `primus`: Founding or original generation.
  * `cursoris`: Associated with cursor movement or pointer dynamics.
  * `caudatus`: Possessing a physical or responsive tail.
  * `electro-mechanica`: Driven by physical switches, cams, or relays.
  * `vulnerabilis`: Highly fragile, needy, or non-utilitarian design.
